# Problem 10 — Storefront design

The storefront now uses a more distinctive Campus Customs visual system: deep Yale blue provides trust and structure, baby pink signals warmth, and pearl-white surfaces keep a large catalogue feeling light. A refined editorial serif is paired with a clean system sans serif so product names feel special while prices, filters, sizes, and navigation remain easy to scan.

The navigation was tightened into a clearer retail header with an original Yale/New Haven seal, pill-style active states, and stronger account actions. The home hero now feels like a campaign rather than a template, using layered real catalogue photography, a collection label, an original seal, pearl texture, and restrained bow details without relying on generated artwork.

Product cards gained richer image framing, clearer hierarchy, improved price placement, softer depth, and a bow reveal on hover. Product pages now present colors as readable chips, emphasize selected sizes, show total availability and local pickup, and keep the product image prominent and sticky on larger screens. Buttons use subtle lift and light-sweep feedback so actions feel responsive without distracting motion.

The chat was restyled as the Campus Customs concierge with the same navy, pink, pearl, typography, and bow language as the shop. Suggested starter questions help shoppers begin quickly, while branded loading and retry states make the assistant feel like part of the buying journey. Responsive layouts preserve readable spacing, touch targets, filters, cards, product details, and chat behavior from wide screens down to phones.

These changes should help customers stay longer and buy by making products easier to understand, giving high-intent actions more visual weight, reducing uncertainty around availability, and keeping discovery consistent whether it starts in the catalogue or the concierge.
